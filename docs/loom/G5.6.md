# G5.6 Pins & Pinnings in Tapestry

Tapestry builds the Pins & Pinnings draft ([`protocols/drafts/pins.md`](../../protocols/drafts/pins.md), Draft 3, a pre-NIP). A person, or an agent working for them, pins a DList or a Tag to a named Pin, one Pin for each reason, and each Pin carries what follows from it. The Pins and the pinnings are made in the graph first, as the instance's own state (BIBLE §30), and go out as letters only when the instance publishes them. G5.7 then brings the draft to what this builds. The alert Pin and the alerts themselves are G6.4's, and the alert Pin's entry in the draft is G6.5's.

How the pieces fit was an architectural choice, so an ADR came before the stories: [ADR pins-and-pinnings 0001](../../engineering-team/decisions/pins-and-pinnings/0001-pins-and-pinnings-in-the-graph.md), which the owner accepted on 2026-11-22. The stories follow it, under "Stories".

## The problem

Today Tapestry has one kind of pin, the `tag-pinning` concept ([`protocols/drafts/tags.md`](../../protocols/drafts/tags.md) § Pins):

- **A pin is made in the browser.** It is a kind-`39999` event the pinner signs with their own key, sent to the instance's relay and to public relays (`pinTag` in `ui/src/utils/publishTagPin.js`). Nothing writes it to the graph, and no graph operation makes one, so an agent working through the external CLI can't pin.
- **A pin always means one thing:** publish Trusted Lists for this Tag. The instance's Assistant signs a kind-`30392` list of the people the Tag is used on and a kind-`30393` list of the notes (`src/api/trustedList/refreshPinnedTags.js`). How each is curated rides in the pin itself, as its `curation-method` JSON. The same click can also sign NIP-51 lists of those people and notes under the pinner's own key.
- **Only a Tag can be pinned.** A DList header can't, so nothing can say "show this list in my Dictionary" or "tell me when this list gets a new item".
- **Every pin is public, and there is no `−`.** Unpinning is a NIP-09 deletion. It carries no `z`, so it never reaches readers who find pins by their concept (ledger `2026-09-27-revokes-do-not-travel`). Pins are also counted for other people, in the Tags directory's "Most pinned" sort, and nothing tells the pinner.

David wants more than one reason to pin, and only the first exists:

- **`spawns-trusted-list`**, for the Tags whose Trusted Lists he tracks. That is what tag-pinning does today.
- **`add-to-dictionary`**, for the DLists his Dictionary page shows. The page's Add and Veto buttons are stubbed until these pinnings exist ([`docs/DICTIONARY_PAGE_HANDOFF.md`](../DICTIONARY_PAGE_HANDOFF.md) § 3).
- **An alert Pin**, for DLists such as the vulnerability list on david.tapestry.ninja, whose new items should reach him (G6.4).

He also wants Pins that trigger nothing, whose meaning is only that he chose them (P-163). His intake entry of 2026-09-19, "Categories of pins" ([`engineering-team/stories/_intake.md`](../../engineering-team/stories/_intake.md)), asked for the same split by purpose. Show and Tell names why it matters beyond the feature: a pin made for oneself and counted for other people is the reuse hazard, and a Pin per purpose lets the pinner say which reading they endorse ([`design-philosophies/show-and-tell.md`](../../design-philosophies/show-and-tell.md), E12, Q3).

## Who it is for

- **The owner of a Tapestry instance,** starting with David on david.tapestry.ninja.
- **The agents that work for an owner** through the external CLI, such as Zeus and Grok Tapestry Lead. An agent pins with the same graph operations the owner's UI uses.
- **People signed in to a shared instance,** such as tapestry.brainstorm.world, who pin Tags there today. Their pins keep working while they move to the new form.

## What it does

1. **The pin concept and the first Pins, in the graph.** The `pin` concept, whose items are Pins, and the `nostr-pinning` concept, whose items are pinnings, are made local-first, with the two well-known Pins this book uses: `spawns-trusted-list` and `add-to-dictionary` (§ 8.1). A Pin is its own list header (§ 2), and anyone may make one (§ 1), so G6.4's alert Pin, and Pins that trigger nothing, are made the same way.
2. **Pinnings.** A person pins a DList header or a Tag to a Pin (`+`), excludes it (`−`), or unpins it (§ 3, § 4), from Tapestry's UI or the external CLI. Each owner has one live stance per Pin and target, named by the draft's `d` tag (§ 3.1). Publishing sends a pinning as a kind-`39999` letter with the draft's tags.
3. **Private pinnings.** An owner can keep a pinning private (§ 9). A private pinning counts only for its owner, never toward anyone else's community, and a visitor to the instance can't read it.
4. **What counts as pinned.** For each Pin, the owner's own pinnings decide (§ 6.1), worked out when it is read, never stored as a result (architecture invariant 3).
5. **Tag-pinning moves onto `spawns-trusted-list`** (§ 10). Pinning a Tag writes the new form. Readers count old and new pins together until the old ones have moved, and every pinned Tag's Trusted Lists keep spawning.
6. **The Dictionary page's Add and Veto** become `add-to-dictionary` pinnings, so the first new Pin has a use the day it ships.

## What done looks like

- the pin concept and its first Pins are in the graph, made local-first;
- a person can pin and unpin a DList or a Tag, publicly or privately;
- tag-pinning's pins have moved onto them (§ 10), and their Trusted Lists still spawn;
- an agent has pinned through the external CLI.

## Out of scope

- **The alert Pin and the alerts** (G6.4), and the alert Pin's entry in the draft (G6.5).
- **`spawns-score`, `spawns-concept` and the display Pins,** such as `profile-page-tags`, except where moving today's pins needs one.
- **Pinning contexts** (§ 3, pending review). Pinnings are generic, as the draft's interim rule says, except where the ADR decides that a moved pin keeps the meaning it has today.
- **Trusted Lists spawned from DList headers.** `spawns-trusted-list` takes Tags in this book.
- **Private pinnings for people who pin in the browser,** which would need NIP-44 in their own signer. The instance's pinnings, made through the graph, can be private.
- **The community's pinnings in an owner's effective set** (§ 6.1's `community(X)`), and counting pinnings for other people, such as the Dictionary's GUM₃ and the Tags directory's "Most pinned". Each is a reuse, so it waits for its own answers to the reuse hazard's three questions.
- **Brainstorm's Trusted List rules,** P-145 and P-146, for brainstorm_server (E9). They lean on the same wire format, so the ADR is shown to Vinney.
- **Changing the draft.** G5.7 brings `pins.md` to what this builds, including any change building it brings to Draft 3.

## What the ADR decides

The draft says what goes on the wire. These are the choices it leaves to Tapestry, or leaves open, and ADR 0001 decides each one:

1. **Where the pin concept and the well-known Pins live, and under whose key.** The draft has deployments seed them "under their authority key" (§ 8.1), but which header is canonical across deployments is worksheet W1, still open (§ 11, question 7). Firmware under each instance's own Assistant (TA) key is what every firmware concept does today. One set under a held key, as the Duty Menu header is (`duty-menus-and-self-maps` ADR 0002), is the other road.
2. **How a Pin and its pinnings sit in the graph.** A Pin is both an item of `pin` and the header of its own pinnings (§ 2). The graph classes every kind-`39999` as a `ListItem`, and links an item to a list only through a kind-`39998` header's supersets or a set's `n` tag (`src/operations/catalog/import/_letters.js`), so a Pin has no form there yet. A pinning's target, polarity and private mark need one too.
3. **Whose a pinning is.** A pinning made through the graph is the instance's own, so the TA signs its letter when export nostr publishes it (BIBLE §31, the first-person rule). Export sends whatever tag nodes a node holds, but the element writes give a node only `d`, `name`, `z`, `n` and `json` today, not the tags a pinning needs. A pin made in the browser is signed by the person who pins. The ADR says which writers go through the graph, and how a reader counts a person's own signed pinning beside the instance's.
4. **What "private" means here.** Leaving a pinning unpublished isn't enough on its own, since visitors can read nodes that were never published (`docs/GRAPH_OPERATIONS_CATALOG.md` § 10, question 1, still open). The draft's form (W22) publishes a private pinning NIP-44-encrypted to the Assistant itself, with `["private", "1"]`. The ADR says how a private pinning is kept from visitors, and whether its letter goes out.
5. **How an unpin is written:** a deletion, `polarity 0`, or both (§ 4; § 11, question 2).
6. **How tag-pinning moves** (§ 10):
   - Today's pins carry target types, people and notes. A moved pin keeps that meaning only through contexts, against the interim generic-only rule.
   - Each pin's curation settings need a home once `curation-method` leaves the wire (§ 7).
   - Whether a pinned Tag's Trusted Lists keep their addresses, and whether the pinner's NIP-51 exports carry on.
   - How readers union the old pins, which stay under ADR 0015's legacy z-tag key, with the new ones.
7. **The operations.** Operations for one kind of concept sit in an app layer above the generic module (`docs/GRAPH_OPERATIONS_CATALOG.md`, "The app layer"). The ADR names the operations that make a Pin, pin, unpin, list the Pins and read what is pinned, so the UI, the routes and the external CLI share one declaration (`graph-operations-foundation` ADR 0001).

## Notes

If the ADR puts the pin concepts in firmware, they join those files before the first release is cut, or go out in a later release.
- **Readers outside Tapestry.** P-163 left unchecked whether Brainstorm's UI reads pins the way Tapestry does. This Mac's copy of brainstorm_server (main, 2026-10-26) reads no pins. Once Tapestry writes the new form, Brainstorm-UI sees no new pins until its reader takes the new form too, so the ADR says whether Tapestry writes both forms for a while, or Brainstorm-UI's reader changes first, as a follow-up in that repo.
- **The stories** are under "Stories", below. P-163 suggested three: the pin concept and its first Pins in the graph; pinnings with `+` and `−` and the private option; and tag-pinning moved onto them. Built as the ADR decides, the second is two stories, pinning through the graph (G5.6.b) and private pinnings (G5.6.d), and the third is three: the readers (G5.6.c), the Tag and DList pages (G5.6.e) and `pin move` (G5.6.f). ## Stories

### G5.6.a The pin concepts and their first two Pins, in the graph

The `pin` and `nostr-pinning` concepts as firmware under each instance's own Assistant, and the first two Pins, `spawns-trusted-list` (for Tags) and `add-to-dictionary` (for DList headers), as firmware elements of `pin`, each at `d` = its slug with the Pins draft's § 2 tags (ADR pins-and-pinnings 0001, Decisions 1 and 2). For them, a firmware element file takes a fixed `d` and tags, which both installs honour; the helpers the element writes share take a fixed `d` and extra tags; and both made-elsewhere lists take the two concepts, so the generic writes refuse their elements and name the pin operation to use. The `pin` group of operations starts here, with `pin list` and `pin make`, so an agent lists the Pins and makes one of its own, such as the alert Pin G6.4 needs, through the external CLI. Deployments need a firmware reinstall once it lands. Done when, on a stack running it with its firmware reinstalled, `pin list` shows the two Pins under the instance's Assistant, a Pin that `pin make` makes goes out through `export nostr` with its § 2 tags, and `graph create-element` and `graph update-node` refuse an element of either concept, naming `pin make` or `pin set`.

### G5.6.b Pin, exclude and unpin through the graph
- depends_on: G5.6.a

`pin set` puts a DList header or a Tag on a Pin as the instance's own pinning: `+` at polarity 1, `−` at polarity -1, and an unpin at polarity 0, which keeps the node (Decisions 2, 4 and 6). The pinning's `d` is the draft's, `pin-<pinSlug>-<h8>-<owner8>`, its name repeats its `d`, and its `a` names the target, with a `REFERENCES` edge when the target is in the graph. `pin set` writes and never signs: the caller sends a public pinning with `export nostr`, a Pin before its pinnings, and the letter carries no `json` tag, since the `nostr-pinning` header says so. `pin read` gives a Pin's pinnings, or one target's stance on every Pin, by Decision 7's rule: one stance per view, Pin and target, from one source, for the instance's view or a person's, read from the graph and the relay with old pins included. The rule is built once, here, for `pin read` and for the readers the later stories change. Done when an agent pins, excludes and unpins a DList header and a Tag through the external CLI on a stack running it, `export nostr` sends each pinning with the draft's tags and no `json`, and `pin read` gives back the stance that counts, an old pin's until its target has a new-form one.

### G5.6.c Trusted Lists spawn from the new form, and the Tag pages count it
- depends_on: G5.6.b

The daily task and the per-viewer refresh gather pinned Tags from three sources: old pins from the relay, the instance's pinnings from the graph, and people's signed new-form pinnings from the relay, leaving out the owner's own new-form letters and any the instance's Assistant signed (Decision 8). The stance that counts is Decision 7's, by the rule G5.6.b builds. A new-form pinning names its Tag by `a`, and its curation comes from its node, else from the pinner's prefs file under the pinning's `d`, else from today's defaults. A graph source that fails stops the run, so nothing is retracted. The per-viewer refresh counts the instance's pinnings as the owner's, and refreshes a graph pinning by its node. The Pinned tab, the Tag page's pin state and "Most pinned" count by the same stance, each pinner once per Tag. Done when a Tag pinned through `pin set` spawns its two Trusted Lists at today's `tl-pin-` addresses, unpinning it at polarity 0 retracts them, an old pin keeps spawning until a new-form stance replaces it, a failed graph read retracts nothing, and the Pinned tab and "Most pinned" show the stance that counts.

### G5.6.d Private pinnings
- depends_on: G5.6.c

`pin set` makes a pinning private: its target, polarity, context and curation go into one NIP-44 payload the Assistant encrypts to itself, the node is marked `["private", "1"]` with no `a` or `polarity` tag node, and its `d` uses a keyed `h8`, whose derivation this story picks and records in an amendment to ADR 0001 (Decision 5). `export nostr` never signs a node marked private, in any scope, and a private pinning's change entries hold nothing in the clear that its node doesn't. Switching between public and private keeps one live stance, and going private sends the public pinning's polarity 0. While the owner's own public stance on that Pin and target stands on the instance's relay, `pin set` refuses to go private and says why. `pin read` decrypts for its callers, spawning decrypts inside the server, "Most pinned" never counts a private stance, and the stance rule shows a private pinning only to its viewer's own session, so every route built on it does the same. Done when an agent pins a Tag and a DList header privately through the external CLI, `pin read` gives back their stances and the Tag's Trusted Lists spawn, and a visitor reading the concept-graph routes, `/api/neo4j/query`, `/api/tapestry-key` and the change entries finds each pinning's Pin and `d`, but not its target, polarity or curation.

### G5.6.e Pin from a Tag's page and a DList's page
- depends_on: G5.6.d, G5.6.g

The Tag page's Pin button, and a Pin control of the same kind on a DList's page, pick a Pin and pin, exclude or unpin (Decisions 3, 5 and 8). For the owner, the UI calls `pin set` and then `export nostr`, with a private option that says before the click when the Pin's action publishes; before going private, it has the owner sign what clears their own public stance, the old pin's deletion or a polarity 0 of their new-form pinning. Everyone else, admins included, signs the new form in the browser, with the signer G5.6.g builds. Pinning a Tag again in the new form copies the old pin's curation into the person's prefs file under the new `d`, so its Trusted Lists keep their addresses, and their NIP-51 exports carry on as they are. Done when the owner and a signed-in person who isn't the owner each pin, exclude and unpin a Tag and a DList header from the UI, the owner also privately, and each page then shows the stance that counts.

### G5.6.f The owner's old pins move into the graph
- depends_on: G5.6.c

`pin move` re-mints the owner's old tag pins, and any new-form pinnings the owner signed in other clients, as the instance's own `spawns-trusted-list` pinnings, with their curation, in one auditable act (Decisions 3, 7 and 8; BIBLE §31). It skips any target the instance already has a pinning of on that Pin, public or private, and where the owner has both an old pin and a new-form pinning of one target, it re-mints the new-form one. Once a Tag has moved, its old pin no longer counts, so the owner needn't sign its deletion. Other people's old pins keep counting until they pin again. Done when, on a stack holding the owner's old pins, `pin move` re-mints each with its curation, their Trusted Lists keep their `tl-pin-` addresses and keep spawning, the old pins stop counting, and a second run writes nothing.

### G5.6.g The Dictionary's Add, Veto and Restore
- depends_on: G5.6.b

The Dictionary page's Add, Veto and Restore become `add-to-dictionary` pinnings of a shared concept's header, each person's own (Decision 9). Add pins it `+` and then does what Add does today, Veto pins it `−`, and Restore unpins it at polarity 0. The owner's go through `pin set` and `export nostr`, and anyone else signs theirs in the browser, with a signer for the new form that the Tag page reuses later (G5.6.e). The Concepts page marks each row added or vetoed from the stance of the person whose Dictionary it is, and the Trusted Dictionary lets a `+`-pinned concept past its evidence check, fetching the concept's header first when the instance's relay lacks it. Done when the owner and a signed-in person who isn't the owner each Add, Veto and Restore a concept on their own Dictionary, the Concepts page shows the mark each step leaves, and the Trusted Dictionary keeps a concept added by hand that no one has filed under.

### G5.6.h Acceptance: the done-when, run on a deployed instance
- kind: record
- depends_on: G5.6.e, G5.6.f

The epic's done-when, run on a deployed instance once its firmware is reinstalled, and written down. The two pin concepts and their first Pins are in its graph. The owner from the UI, and an agent through the external CLI, each pin, exclude and unpin a DList header and a Tag, publicly and privately, and a visitor finds no private pinning's target. `pin move` has moved the owner's old pins, and their Trusted Lists still spawn. Done when the record is on tapestry's `staging`, with what was run and what came back.
